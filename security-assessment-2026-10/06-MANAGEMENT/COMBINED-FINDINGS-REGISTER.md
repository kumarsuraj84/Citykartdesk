## 1. How to read this

This is the one register that ties every security finding in `02-VAPT/VAPT-MASTER-REGISTER.md` (70 rows) to the outside frameworks management has asked about: the common weakness list (CWE), the OWASP Top 10 (2021 edition) and the OWASP API Security Top 10 (2023 edition), the controls in ISO/IEC 27001:2022 Annex A, and the SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria. Each row also carries the business risk, root cause, remediation, evidence, an owner role, how well the finding was verified, and what risk remains.

Read it with these cautions:

1. **The mappings are the assessor's provisional judgement.** Every ISO and SOC 2 reference is marked PROVISIONAL: it must be confirmed against the licensed ISO/IEC 27001:2022 text and the AICPA Trust Services Criteria by someone qualified. Where the fit is especially uncertain the row says "for specialist review". A mapping tells you which control topics a finding touches. It does not say that the control passes or fails.
2. **Status, severity and evidence are copied from the master register**, which copies the lead tester's status table. Nothing was upgraded. Section 2.1 of the master register shows the counts.
3. **Nothing from this assessment is deployed.** Production runs build `20261015-145217` (main `717e5c6`). "Fixed" means fixed in the LOCAL working copy and proven on the isolated test stack only.
4. **Owner roles are placeholders.** The repository does not show who owns what. Management must name the people (see the questions at the end).
5. **No organisational evidence has been supplied** (evidence class C: approved policies, access reviews, restore records, training records, management approvals). None is claimed anywhere in this file.

## 2. A technical fix does not by itself prove ISO 27001 or SOC 2 compliance

This register is built from technical testing. That is useful evidence, but it is not what either standard certifies:

- **ISO/IEC 27001 certifies a management system, not a list of fixed bugs.** An accredited certification body looks for a defined scope, a risk assessment and risk treatment plan, a Statement of Applicability, approved policies, named owners, internal audits, management review and records that the controls actually ran over time. A fixed vulnerability shows that one technical control exists in one build. It does not show that the control is run, monitored, reviewed, or applied across the whole scope.
- **SOC 2 is an attestation by an independent CPA firm.** It covers the criteria the organisation chooses (Security is always included; availability, confidentiality, processing integrity and privacy are optional) and, for a Type 2 report, evidence that controls operated throughout a period (for example dated access reviews and approved changes). This assessment cannot issue or replace that.
- **A fix that is not deployed protects nobody.** Every fix here is LOCAL. For audit purposes a fix also needs a change record, an approval, deployment evidence and a post-deployment check (see the change-management criteria CC8.1 and Annex A control A.8.32).
- **A gap here may be offset, and a strength here may be undermined, by things this assessment cannot see:** a compensating control run by the infrastructure team, a missing policy, an unreviewed administrator list. The repository cannot show those.
- **Open and decision-pending findings are treatment decisions, not just tasks.** Under ISO clause 6.1.3 and 8.3 (PROVISIONAL) management decides to treat, accept, transfer or avoid each risk and records that decision. None of those records exists yet.

In short: use this register to see where technical controls are weak and what to fix first. Do not use it to say that Citykart Desk is, or is not, "compliant" with ISO 27001 or SOC 2.
