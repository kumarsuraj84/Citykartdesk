# SOC 2 Exception Register - CKDesk

Date: 2026-10-15
Status: DRAFT for management review — internal readiness assessment, not an audit report or attestation

## How to read this

An exception is a case where a control does not exist, did not run, or ran late or incompletely. A SOC 2 Type II auditor will find exceptions on their own; what matters is that the organisation **already knows about them, has recorded them, has a plan, and handles new ones in the same way**. This register has two parts: (1) the real, known exceptions found in the repository and in the 2026-10 VAPT, all identified on 2026-10-15, and (2) empty rows for exceptions found in future.

Rules for this register:

- Only exceptions that are real and supported by the repository or the VAPT are pre-loaded (the last column says where each comes from). Nothing is invented and no exception may be back-dated: the "Date identified" is the day the organisation first wrote it down.
- **Compensating controls** listed are only those that really exist according to the repository. "None confirmed" is a valid and honest answer. A compensating control is not a fix; the auditor decides whether it is enough.
- **Owner** and **target** are placeholders. Owner: to be assigned by management. The target dates are suggestions for discussion, not commitments.
- Fixes made in the LOCAL assessment branch are **not deployed**. Production runs build 20261015-145217 (main 717e5c6), which does not contain them. An exception stays Open until the fix is live in production and evidence is kept.
- Severity words in the Impact column follow the VAPT status table where one exists; otherwise they are the assessor's reading.
- Controls refer to C01-C16 in SOC2-TYPE-II-CONTROL-OPERATION-PLAN.md. Mapping to Trust Services Criteria is PROVISIONAL — confirm against the licensed standard.

## Exception handling steps (summary)

1. Anyone who notices a control did not run, ran late, or failed adds a row the same day.
2. The control owner proposes impact and a plan within 5 working days (PROVISIONAL target).
3. The Information Security Lead reviews new rows weekly and reports open items monthly to the Executive Sponsor.
4. Exceptions that cannot be fixed within the agreed time need a signed risk acceptance with an end date (see C05 and C13 in the operation plan).
5. Closing needs evidence (for example the restore test record) and a second person's confirmation.
6. All rows, open and closed, are kept for the examination period plus 3 years (PROVISIONAL) and shown to the auditor on request.
